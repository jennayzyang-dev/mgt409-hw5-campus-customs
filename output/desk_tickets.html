Reflection

Based on the real runs in the Ticket 101 / 102 / 103 tabs and the Cash tab (run on 2026-10-15, business date 2026-08-31)

1. How I'd evaluate the agents on each ticket

Ticket 101 (Tauhid's tee): B+
The agents did the main job right. Inventory checked and found 0 white tees in size S. It also saw the reprint was already ordered on invoice 501, so it didn't order another one, which was good. Accounting prepared exactly $840 and said the till would go from $3,400 to $2,560. Customer service wrote Tauhid an honest reply. It didn't promise a date and didn't tell him we hadn't paid the vendor. Facilities wasn't called, which is correct because there's no lease here.

But I took points off because:

  • Inventory and accounting went back and forth asking each other "does not paying invoice 501 stop the shipment?" Our database can't answer that (there's no shipping info), so those calls were wasted.
  • Accounting changed its mind. First it said it wouldn't prepare the payment, then it prepared it a few steps later.
  • It was expensive for 1 tee: 5 handoffs, 23 model requests, about 44k input / 6k output tokens.

Ticket 102 (rent notice): A
This was the best one. Only 3 agents, 3 handoffs, 14 model requests (about 26k / 3k tokens). Facilities did what I wanted. It didn't just trust the email that said "rent due in 2 days." It checked lease 1: $2,400 to Elm City Properties, due 2026-09-02, not paid yet, so urgent. Accounting prepared the rent as action 2. When the boss asked again, accounting saw it was already prepared and didn't make a second one. The only waste was the boss asking accounting twice.

Ticket 103 (AI Club hoodies): C+
The numbers were really good:

  • Stock: only 8 of 20 in size M, so we're short 12.
  • Price: cost $22, list $58. 10% off is $52.20, still above our 2× cost rule ($44). For 20 that's $1,044 instead of $1,160.
  • Restock: inventory prepared an order for the 12 missing hoodies, $264 from Bulldog Print Co, which takes 5 days (about 9/5).

But there were 2 big problems:

  • Customer service broke my rule. Its reply to the club already offers $52.20, but my prompt says customer service can only quote list price, and a person has to approve discounts. The boss told it "no owner exception required" because the discount was "within policy." But within policy is not the same as approved. In a real shop that's $116 of margin promised to a student group without the owner saying yes.
  • It was the most expensive ticket: 7 handoffs, 28 model requests, about 59k / 10k tokens. Customer service went back to inventory and accounting to check things they had already reported.

What I noticed:

  • I picked the right agents on all 3 tickets. No ticket used every agent, and facilities was only used on rent.
  • What I got wrong was how they talk. I thought the boss would hand off to each agent and they'd report back. In real life the agents also talked to each other: inventory to accounting on 101, facilities to accounting on 102, customer service to both on 103.
  • On 103 the boss went to the discount first, probably because the ticket subject says "bulk hoodie discount." My plan said check stock first, since there's no point pricing 20 when we only have 8.
  • In my plan I wrote record_payment, but we changed the app after Problem 6 so agents can only prepare a payment and a person approves it. That's why the Cash tab only has one money change: I approved invoice 501, so $3,400 − $840 = $2,560. Rent and the hoodie order are still waiting.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

I think 102 and 101 would be easier and cheaper with just one agent.

  • 102 is really only 3 tool calls: get_lease(1), list_payments, then prepare_payment for $2,400. We used 3 agents and 14 requests. One agent could do it in about 3–4 requests and get the same result: the same $2,400 slip in the sign-off tray.
  • 101 is the same: check_inventory, get_invoice(501), prepare_payment, then write the reply. A lot of the 23 requests were agents reading the same ticket again and calling get_today / get_ticket again, because each agent starts from zero.

The main reason is that our safety rules are not in the agents. They're in the MCP server and the approve button: exact amount, rent only within 7 days, two people to approve, no duplicates. So one agent with the same tools would be just as safe, because it still can't spend money by itself, and it would cost much less.

A team only makes sense when a ticket needs different skills, like 103 (stock + pricing + customer reply). But 103 also shows the problem with a team: the discount rule got lost when the boss passed the message on. The boss said "no exception needed," and customer service believed it. One agent that saw all the facts itself might not make that mistake.

4. Three new problems the team could solve with the tools we have

  1. Out-of-stock check and restock. The Crest Mug (CC-MUG-CREST) has 0 in stock, and so does the white tee size S. Inventory can use list_inventory and list_vendors, see that mugs come from Elm City Gifts (3 days) and shirts from Bulldog Print Co (5 days), and use prepare_purchase. For example, 24 mugs × $3.50 = $84 goes to the sign-off tray, like our $264 hoodie order. Accounting can check the till first.
  2. Another club wants a lot of Yale Caps. CC-HAT-BLUE has 40 in stock, cost $6, list $22. Inventory checks stock, and accounting checks the 10% rule: $19.80, still above the $12 minimum. Customer service writes the reply, and this time quotes list price until a person approves the discount (the lesson from 103). The tools are check_inventory, get_pricing and update_ticket.
  3. Cash check before I approve more bills. Right now the till is $2,560, and rent ($2,400, action 2) and the hoodie order ($264, action 3) are still waiting. Accounting can use get_cash_balance, list_pending_actions, get_lease and list_payments to tell me: "if you pay rent before 9/2 you'll have $160, which is under the $300 line, so wait on the hoodie bill." That's the same math as my Cash tab, but done by the agent.

5. Three new problems the team could not solve, and what we'd need

  1. "When will Tauhid's tee actually arrive?" This was the open question on 101. Our database has no order date, no shipping status and no vendor contact. That's why inventory and accounting kept asking each other about invoice 501. We would need:
    • a purchase_orders table with order date, ship date and tracking number
    • tools like get_po_status and request_vendor_update to ask Bulldog Print Co
    • a Vendor agent to handle talking to vendors
    Then customer service could give Tauhid a real date instead of "we'll follow up."
  2. Actually selling the hoodies to the AI Club (take a deposit, hold the 8 hoodies, record the sale). Right now nothing can hold stock: the inventory number never changes. There's no orders or sales table, and money only goes out of the till, never in. Someone else could buy those 8 hoodies tomorrow. We would need:
    • orders and sales tables
    • tools: reserve_stock, record_sale (adds money to the till) and issue_refund
    • prepare_discount, so a discount becomes a slip that a person has to approve, like payments. That would have stopped the $52.20 problem on 103.
    • a Sales agent to handle orders
  3. Actually sending the messages, and checking with the landlord. Customer service wrote good replies to Tauhid, the AI Club and Elm City Properties, but nothing sends them. There's no email in the tickets table and no outbox. Also, if the rent email were a scam asking us to pay a new bank account, facilities could see it doesn't match the lease but couldn't call the landlord to check. We would need:
    • a contacts table and a send_message tool that only sends after a person clicks "Send," like our approve button
    • a tool to log landlord calls
    • a Communications agent in charge of the outbox
    That way a person still checks anything that leaves the shop, the same as we did with money.
